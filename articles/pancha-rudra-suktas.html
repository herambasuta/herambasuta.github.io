Pancha Rudra Suktas

22 April 2026

The five suktas devoted principally to Rudra in the Ṛgveda are traditionally called the Pañca Rudra Sūktāni. They span four Maṇḍalas and represent the earliest systematic Vedic portrait of Rudra as both a terrifying deity and a supremely benevolent physician-healer.

Sukta Opening Deity (Devatā) Ṛṣi (Seer) Chandas
RV 1.43 Kadrudrāya Rudra Kaṇva Ghaura Gāyatrī
RV 1.114 Imā Rudrāya Rudra Kutsa Āṅgirasa Jagatī
RV 2.33 Āte Pitar Marutām Rudra Gṛtsamada Śaunaka Triṣṭup
RV 6.74 Soma-Rudra Soma and Rudra (Somārudrau) Bharadvāja Bārhaspatya Triṣṭup
RV 7.46 Imā Rudrāya Sthiradhanvane Rudra Vasiṣṭha Maitrāvaruṇī Triṣṭup

The single most dominant theme across all five hymns is the coexistence of Rudra's fearsome destructive power with his supreme healing grace. Every sukta balances a description of Rudra's terrible weapons (heti, śarya, dhanus) with an enumeration of his medicines (bheṣajāni). The seers do not resolve this paradox - they hold it as the defining truth of Rudra's nature.

7.46.2 establishes Rudra's dual kingship - over earth (kṣamya) and heaven (divya). RV 2.33.9 calls him 'Īśāna' (the Sovereign, Overlord) - a name that becomes one of Śiva's aspects. 2.33.10 declares 'there is none mightier than thee, O Rudra'.

Across 1.114.6 and 2.33.1, Rudra is repeatedly addressed as 'Pitar Marutām' - Father of the Maruts. This fatherhood is to be noted: the violent weather-phenomena (storms, lightning, thunder) are the children of Rudra. When devotees pray to the Maruts, they are in fact approaching Rudra's extended family. This explains why petitions to Rudra are often framed as family appeals.

In 6.74.4, Soma-Rudra are asked to liberate the devotee from 'Varuṇa's bonds' (varuṇasya pāśāt). (Ref. Varuṇa’s main role is to maintain ṛta-the cosmic and moral order that controls both nature and human behavior. He is described as a watchful guardian of truth who punishes those who do wrong but forgives those who sincerely repent). Rudra-Soma's healing thus functions simultaneously as moral liberation - sin is the disease, and the god's grace is both the medicine and the release from bondage. This idea becomes the heart of Śaiva liberation theology in the Āgamas.

RV 7.46.3 gives Rudra 'a thousand life-giving medicines' (sahasraṃ bheṣajā), and RV 1.43.4 calls him 'Jalāṣabheṣaja' (he whose medicines confer joy). This defines Rudra's role as Vaidyanātha (Lord of Physicians) - a role fully mainstreamed in the Śrī Rudram of the Yajurveda where the healing aspect is elaborated in minute detail across the anuvakas.

Some of the names used for Rudra are Gāthāpati (lord of sacred songs), Medhāpati (lord of yajna), Kapardī (the one with matted hair), Īśāna, Bhiṣaktama, Babhru (reddish brown coloured), Vajrabāhu, Sthiradhanvan (He of the firm bow), Pracetasa (the intelligent one), Sahamāna (victorious), Kṣayadvīra (destroyer of heroes), Mīḍhuṣṭama (giver of boons), Vṛṣabha and many more.
